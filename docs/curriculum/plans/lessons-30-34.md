# Lesson plan 30–34

For the owner's sign-off. Once approved, content builds lessons 30 to 34 from this file, one per run, in the lesson format (`content.js` header) and under `docs/specs/lesson-experience.md`: items are labelled word or phrase, learners only pick or build words already taught, whole short sentences are typed from lesson 11 on with the word bank one tap away. Facts come from `docs/curriculum/story.md` (and the outline rows for 30–34 in #122); anything this plan adds is marked **new fact** and goes into the continuity log when the lesson merges.

Owner rule holds everywhere: nothing religious or political. The walk in lesson 30 passes a department store, a fountain and a station; no church, no monument to anyone. Every headword below was checked against lessons 1–15 and plans 16–29, so none repeats; each lesson has 12–13 items (phrases counted).

Lessons 30–34 are the middle of chapter 3, **"Unterwegs"**. They follow the course out into the city and back to Neukölln: the walk after the phone-free morning (30), a week of shopping for a winter jacket (31–32) and a dinner for the class that Yuki cooks (33–34). Lesson 32 is the **review stop** (after 4, 8, … 32): its scene samples words from lessons 29–32. Lesson 34 ends on the chapter's cliffhanger: Lena is hurt at football, the setup for lessons 35–36.

## Lesson 30: Places on the way

- **Station:** Wittenbergplatz (second lesson of the topic). **Grammar:** answering *Wo ist …?* with a place word and no preposition: *hier, da, dort* (lessons 5, 18), now *da vorne, oben, unten, gegenüber*. *Das Kaufhaus ist gegenüber.* *Oben ist ein Café.* Plus *Wie findest du …?* as a fixed question for an opinion, answered *Ich finde es schön* (*finden* from lesson 29, with a second meaning). **ES contrast:** *enfrente* is *gegenüber*, and it can stand alone at the end of the sentence like *enfrente*. *¿Qué te parece el edificio?* is *Wie findest du das Gebäude?*: German uses "find", so *Ich finde es schön* means "I think it's beautiful", not that you found it.
- **Words (13):** das Haus, das Gebäude, das Kaufhaus, der Brunnen, das Dach, hoch, schön, hässlich, gegenüber, oben, unten (words); *da vorne*, *Wie findest du …?* (phrases, parts shown).
- **Warm-up (lessons 4, 10, 29):** neu, alt, geradeaus, die Ecke.
- **Scene:** Thursday, 10:50, the café at Viktoria-Luise-Platz (lesson 29). Lena comes back in from her call and sits down. Everyone looks at her. *Und?* She says only *Vielleicht. Noch nicht.* (lessons 8 and 12), and puts the phone in her pocket, not face down. Weber: *So. Zurück zum Wittenbergplatz.* On the walk back Ana walks in front and, now that the class knows what she is (lesson 25), she talks about every building: the fountain on the square, a house that is *alt* with a roof that is *neu*, a new building she finds *hässlich* and says so. The learner asks the way to things (*Wo ist der Bahnhof?* — *Da vorne.*) and gives an opinion when Ana asks *Wie findest du das Haus?* At Wittenbergplatz: the old station building, and *gegenüber* the big department store. Weber, who has an opinion on everything: *Oben ist das Essen. Sehr gut. Sehr teuer.* (teuer is heard and glossed here, taught in 32).
- **Who:** Weber, Ana, Lena, Tomasz, Yuki, the learner.
- **Twist:** at the station, Ana counts the class out of habit (thread 5): *Sechs. Wo ist Tomasz?* Tomasz is there, but he is staring at an empty bike rack. His bike is still at Zoo, one stop back: he walked the whole way with the class. Weber looks at his watch, then at Tomasz: *Dienstag, 9:00. Mit dem Fahrrad?* Tomasz: *Mit dem Fahrrad.* (lesson 28). **New facts:** Tomasz left his bike at Bahnhof Zoo; Lena's answer from the club is still *vielleicht* after the three calls (thread 2; the yes stays in 39). Payoff in the first line of lesson 31.
- **Change from the outline:** the outline has Tomasz late again on Tuesday in this lesson. The lesson ends on Thursday instead, with the bike left behind, and Tuesday's *Die U9 war spät* opens lesson 31, so each lesson keeps one day.

## Lesson 31: Clothes

- **Station:** Kurfürstendamm. **Grammar:** the accusative with *der*, *die* and *das*: only *der* changes, to *den*, as *ein* changed to *einen* in lesson 14. *Ich nehme den Mantel. Ich nehme die Jacke. Ich nehme das Hemd.* Plus *tragen* (*du trägst, er trägt*: a→ä like *fahren*, lesson 27). **ES contrast:** *llevar* means both "wear" and "carry", and so does *tragen*: *Ich trage eine Jacke*, *Ich trage die Tasche*. Here Spanish helps and English misleads. Gender does not follow Spanish: *la chaqueta* is *die Jacke*, but *la camisa* is *das Hemd* and *el abrigo* is *der Mantel*.
- **Words (13):** die Jacke, der Mantel, der Pullover, die Hose, das Hemd, das T-Shirt, der Schuh, der Schal, die Mütze, tragen, warm, dünn (words); *für alle Fälle* (phrase, parts shown).
- **Warm-up (lessons 12, 13, 17):** kalt, zu, groß, klein.
- **Scene:** Tuesday, 12:45, after class. It is the first really cold day. Tomasz came to class twenty minutes late: *Die U9 war spät.* (the bike is still at Zoo, lesson 30; thread 5). Weber: *Die U9. Natürlich.* On the way out Ana touches the learner's jacket: *Deine Jacke ist zu dünn. Das ist Berlin, {name}. Nicht {city}.* She and Lena take the learner to a second-hand shop on Karl-Marx-Straße that Ana knows. The learner says what they want (*Ich brauche einen Mantel*, lesson 14's *brauchen* with the new *den*), tries things on, and the two give opinions: Ana measures each sleeve with her hand (lessons 14, 17, 25), Lena is blunt (*Der Schal ist hässlich*, lesson 30). Nothing fits yet. The owner, in German: *Am Donnerstag kommen neue Jacken.*
- **Who:** Ana, Lena, the learner, the shop owner (two or three lines, unnamed for now), Tomasz and Weber in the intro.
- **Twist:** Lena said she needs nothing, and then she comes out of the changing room in a black shirt and buys it at once. Ana: *Ein Hemd? Warum?* Lena: *Nur für alle Fälle.* Ana: *Welche Fälle?* Lena: *Nächste Frage, Ana.* (the class's dodge from lessons 7 and 10). The learner can see the shirt is for a stage (thread 2: still unsaid). **New facts:** it is cold in Berlin now and the learner's jacket is too thin; Ana knows a second-hand shop on Karl-Marx-Straße; Lena owns a black stage shirt (she wears it at the gig, lesson 46).
- **Change from the outline:** none. *warm* is taught here (a jacket is *warm*), so the weather lesson (41) recalls it instead of introducing it.

## Lesson 32: Prices and sizes (review stop)

- **Station:** Kurfürstendamm (second lesson of the topic). **Grammar:** *ihn, sie, es* for a thing you already named: *Der Mantel? Ich nehme ihn. Die Jacke? Ich nehme sie.* (*den* becomes *ihn*, the same *n*, lesson 31). *Was kostet …?* for one thing, *Was kosten …?* for more (*kosten*, lesson 16). *zu* + adjective (lesson 12): *zu eng, zu kurz, zu teuer*. Prices read as on the tag: *neunzehn Euro neunzig*. **ES contrast:** *me lo llevo* is *Ich nehme ihn*: Spanish puts *lo* before the verb, German puts *ihn* after it. *lang* is long (a sleeve), *lange* is a long time (lesson 27).
- **Words (13):** die Größe, passen, ihn, teuer, billig, günstig, der Preis, die Kasse, lang, kurz, eng (words); *Das steht dir*, *Bar oder mit Karte?* (phrases, parts shown). Note on the card: *billig* can also mean "cheap and bad"; *günstig* is the polite word.
- **Warm-up (lessons 15, 16, 31):** kosten, der Euro, die Karte, die Jacke.
- **Scene:** Thursday, 12:45, the same shop. The new jackets came in. Lena is late and phones from the station: *Wo seid ihr?* The learner gives her the way (*Geradeaus, dann links. Wir sind da vorne.*, lessons 29–30), and while they wait Ana asks what the learner thinks of the building across the street (*Wie findest du das Haus?*, lesson 30): the review questions. In the shop the owner asks *Welche Größe?* The first jacket is *zu eng*, the second *zu kurz* (Ana measures the sleeve). The third passes. Lena, who never says anything nice: *Das steht dir.* It is the first compliment from her in the course, and the learner hears it. The price on the tag is *neunzehn Euro neunzig*. At the till: *Bar oder mit Karte?* (*die Karte* again: menu in 15, ticket machine in 28, now the till).
- **Who:** Ana, Lena, the learner, the shop owner (named this time: Rodrigo, from Santiago de Chile, 60s).
- **Twist:** Ana, tired at the end of the day, says to the learner in Spanish without thinking: *¡Qué bonita!* (as in lesson 13, *sin … ohne*). The owner looks up and switches to Spanish at once: *¿Mexicana? Para usted, quince.* Ana: *Auf Deutsch, bitte.* (lesson 26, her own rule now). He says it again: *Fünfzehn Euro. Für Sie.* The learner: *Und für mich?* — *Neunzehn neunzig.* The learner, in German, with Murat's line from lesson 11: *Ich höre alles. Auch Spanisch.* He laughs: *Na gut. Fünfzehn.* **New facts:** the learner has a warm jacket from Rodrigo's shop for the rest of the winter; Rodrigo is from Chile and has run the shop on Karl-Marx-Straße for years; Ana now asks for German herself.
- **Review stop:** the eighth (after 4, 8, … 32). The questions on the way sample words from lessons 29–32 (directions, a building, the jacket, the price).
- **Change from the outline:** the outline has Ana answer *Auf Deutsch, bitte* as the whole twist. The beat is the same, and the learner's Spanish turns it into a small win: the learner, who speaks Spanish too, gets the same price by saying so in German, with words they already know.

## Lesson 33: Quantities

- **Station:** Uhlandstraße. **Grammar:** an amount comes straight before the thing, with no "of": *ein Kilo Tomaten, 200 Gramm Käse, eine Packung Reis, eine Flasche Öl, eine Dose Tomaten*. *ein bisschen* (lesson 12) now before a noun: *ein bisschen Salz*. *viel* and *wenig* do not change: *viel Fisch, wenig Zeit*. **ES contrast:** *un kilo de tomates* is *ein Kilo Tomaten*: the *de* disappears; *un poco de* is *ein bisschen*, also with no *de*. And *Gramm* and *Kilo* stay singular after a number: *zwei Kilo*, never *zwei Kilos*.
- **Words (13):** das Kilo, das Gramm, die Packung, die Flasche, die Dose, das Stück, der Fisch, die Nudeln, das Öl, viel, wenig, genug (words); *Das reicht* (phrase, parts shown; Weber said it in lesson 9, now the learner learns it).
- **Warm-up (lessons 14, 16):** die Tomate, der Reis, brauchen, hundert.
- **Scene:** Tuesday, 13:00, the market at Maybachufer (it runs on Tuesdays and Fridays, as in lesson 14). Yuki will cook a Japanese dinner for the class on Thursday at Ana's flat (Ana cooked Mexican there in lesson 17). Yuki's list is exact, in grams, in her small notebook (lesson 22). The learner and Ana go with her and do the talking: *Ein Kilo, bitte. Nein, zwei Kilo.* *Ist das genug?* The stall holder from lesson 14 recognises Ana at once: *Die Köchin!* (his guess in lesson 14). This time Ana answers: *Nein. Ich bin Architektin.* (lesson 25; in 14 she changed the subject). He points at Yuki: *Und Sie?* Yuki: *Ich bin Köchin.* He: *Na endlich.*
- **Who:** Yuki, Ana, the learner, the stall holder from lesson 14.
- **Twist:** Yuki shops for seven: the class (*Wir sind sieben*, lesson 9). Ana, who counts everything (thread 5), counts again and gets eight. *Acht. Murat kommt auch.* Yuki: *Murat? Warum?* Ana: *Er hört alles.* (lesson 11: he heard them plan it at the café and asked *Und ich?*). Yuki looks at her list and adds one more *Stück* of fish. **New facts:** Yuki cooks the dinner for the class on Thursday evening at Ana's; Murat is invited (his first scene outside the café is the dinner, after lesson 34); the stall holder now knows Ana is an architect.
- **Change from the outline:** none. *ein bisschen* is in the outline for this lesson but was taught in lesson 12; it is recalled here before a noun, which frees the slot for *das Stück*.

## Lesson 34: The shopping list

- **Station:** Uhlandstraße (second lesson of the topic). **Grammar:** *kein* in the accusative, like *ein*: *keinen Ingwer, keine Butter, kein Salz* (*kein*, lesson 18; *einen*, lesson 14). With *mehr*: *Es gibt keinen Fisch mehr. Wir haben kein Salz mehr.* **ES contrast:** *ya no hay* is *es gibt … kein … mehr*: Spanish puts *ya no* together at the front, German splits it round the noun. *No hay más* is not *nicht mehr*: with a noun, German says *kein … mehr*.
- **Words (12):** der Supermarkt, kaufen, die Liste, die Tüte, mehr, leer, voll, das Salz, die Butter, der Ingwer, die Sojasoße (words); *Was fehlt noch?* (phrase, parts shown).
- **Warm-up (lessons 12, 18, 33):** es gibt, kein, schwer, die Packung.
- **Scene:** Thursday, 16:30, a supermarket on Karl-Marx-Straße. Yuki is cooking at Ana's from five; Ana and Lena are at football after class, as every Thursday (lesson 6). Yuki opens Ana's kitchen cupboard (lesson 17): the rice packet is *leer*, there is no salt. Ana texts a list from the pitch, in German: *Bitte kaufen: Reis, Salz, Butter, Ingwer, Sojasoße. Lena bringt das Bier.* The learner and Yuki shop it. The learner asks a shop assistant where things are (*Wo ist der Ingwer?* — *Da vorne, links*, lessons 29–30) and hears *Ingwer ist alle. Kein Ingwer mehr.* (Berliners say *alle* for "all gone"; the learner recalls *alle* from lesson 11 in a new sense). Yuki: *Kein Ingwer? Dann kein Sushi.* The learner finds a second shelf with ginger. Two *Tüten*, *voll* and *schwer* (lesson 12). *Was fehlt noch?* The learner texts Lena about the beer: no answer.
- **Who:** Yuki, the learner, a shop assistant (one or two lines), Ana and Lena by message.
- **Twist:** the learner's phone buzzes at the till. It is Ana, not Lena: *Lena ist verletzt. Die Hand. Wir sind am Tempelhofer Feld. Kommst du?* (*verletzt* is heard and glossed; it is taught in lesson 35). Yuki takes one bag: *Ich koche. Du gehst.* The learner runs, with the other bag. Cliffhanger for lesson 35. **New facts:** Lena hurt her hand at football on Thursday; the learner arrives at Tempelhofer Feld carrying a heavy bag (lesson 35: the learner's back); Yuki keeps cooking, so the dinner still happens (lessons 35–36 can mention it).
- **Change from the outline:** none. The list comes from Ana by message (the outline's "Ana texts a list"), and Yuki stays, so the dinner of lesson 33 is not lost.
