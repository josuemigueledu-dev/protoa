# Lesson plan 20–24

For the owner's sign-off. Once approved, content builds lessons 20 to 24 from this file, one per run, in the lesson format (`content.js` header) and under `docs/specs/lesson-experience.md`: items are labelled word or phrase, learners only pick or build words already taught, one typed word per gap item, whole short sentences from lesson 11 with the word bank one tap away. Facts come from `docs/curriculum/story.md`; anything this plan adds is marked **new fact** and goes into the continuity log when the lesson merges.

Owner rule holds everywhere: nothing religious or political. No holidays or festivals appear in the months or the week; Sunday is simply "frei". Every headword below was checked against lessons 1–9 and plans 10–19, so none repeats; each lesson has 13–14 items. *Tag* and *Morgen* were met only as the parts of *Guten Tag* and *Guten Morgen* in lesson 1; here they become words of their own.

Lessons 20–22 finish chapter 2's time block ("Das Café und zu Hause"). Lesson 24 is the **review stop** and the end of chapter 2 (review stops fall after 4, 8, ... 24): its scene samples words from lessons 21–24.

## Lesson 20: Times of day

- **Station:** Alexanderplatz (second lesson of the topic). **Grammar:** *Wann …? Um zehn Uhr. Von neun bis elf.* *um* with a time, *von … bis* for a stretch; *-s* adverbs (*morgens*) are left for A2. *immer, manchmal, nie* sit after the verb: *Tomasz kommt manchmal pünktlich.* **ES contrast:** *a las diez* = *um zehn Uhr* (no article, no "las"); *der Morgen* (morning) is not *morgen* (tomorrow, lesson 7): same letters, capital M, different word; and *Mittag* is midday (12–13), not "afternoon", which is *Nachmittag*.
- **Words (14):** der Morgen, der Vormittag, der Mittag, der Nachmittag, der Abend, die Nacht, um, von, bis, ändern, immer, manchmal, nie, die Zeit.
- **Warm-up (lessons 7, 19):** wann, die Uhr, spät, die Pause.
- **Scene:** Tuesday, 9:00, the hallway outside room 2.04. A note on the door: the course is **10:00 to 12:00** this week only, because the room is needed. Weber is already there, calm. Ana and Lena read the note aloud and each asks *Wann?* The learner works out the new day: the break moves to *halb zwölf*, so the café will be empty when they get there, and Lena asks twice whether she can still make her call at 14:00 (lesson 19; she can). Everyone panics a little, in different ways: Ana asks twice, Lena checks her phone, Tomasz is *pünktlich* for the first time in his life and for once has nothing to say.
- **Who:** Weber, Ana, Lena, Tomasz, the learner.
- **Twist:** at 9:00 sharp Weber's phone rings, as it does every Tuesday. He lifts a hand and opens his mouth; the class says it for him, together: *Einen Moment, bitte.* (callback to lesson 1, where he said it). He smiles for the first time in the course and steps out. **New fact:** he moved the course to ten, not for the room; nobody asks why. (Payoff in lesson 23: the 9:00 call is Clara, and this week it is a long one.) Tomasz notices he was the only one who was early and says it to nobody.
- **Change from the outline:** the outline had Weber change the time and then the phone ring; here the class turns his own phrase back on him, which uses only lesson 1 words.

## Lesson 21: The week

- **Station:** Rosenthaler Platz. **Grammar:** *am Montag* (on Monday), *am Wochenende*; *Welcher Tag ist heute?* as a fixed phrase (not taught as grammar); days are masculine (*der Montag*), always capitalised. **ES contrast:** Spanish *el lunes* is German *am Montag* (no *el*); the week starts on Monday in German; *Sonnabend* is a regional word for Samstag, skip it.
- **Words (13):** der Montag, der Dienstag, der Mittwoch, der Donnerstag, der Freitag, der Samstag, der Sonntag, die Woche, das Wochenende, der Tag, am, frei, der Kalender.
- **Warm-up (lessons 9, 19, 20):** sieben, der Abend, immer, die Pause.
- **Scene:** Thursday, 10:30 (the course is back at 9:00), the café. Murat has put a paper calendar on the wall by the till, because Ana wants to plan the learner's week and Lena's. Football: *Montag*, Ana and Lena. Ana's work: *Dienstag* to *Freitag*, *Samstag* free. The band: *Freitag, Abend*. Course: *Dienstag und Donnerstag*. The learner fills in a week by placing tiles on days, and Ana asks the questions: *Was machst du am Mittwoch?* Murat: closed on *Sonntag*. One day, *Mittwoch*, is empty for the learner; Ana writes *Café, 10:30* in it and Murat nods.
- **Who:** Ana, Lena, the learner, Murat (named since lesson 15).
- **Twist:** Ana writes *Lena* on every day but one. Lena's **Samstag** is empty, and when Ana points at it Lena says: *Nein. Samstag ist frei. Noch.* and turns her phone face down (thread 2). **New fact:** Lena keeps next Saturday empty on purpose; she is waiting for an answer (still unsaid).
- **Change from the outline:** none.

## Lesson 22: The months

- **Station:** Rosenthaler Platz (second lesson of the topic). **Grammar:** *im Mai, im Juni* (in + month); *Wann haben Sie Geburtstag? Ich habe im März Geburtstag.* (idiom with *haben*, lesson 18). **ES contrast:** months are capitalised in German and lower case in Spanish (*mayo* / *Mai*); most months are cognates (*Februar, April, Juli, September*), so the trap is spelling and the *-r* at the end; *März* and *Mai* are shorter than *marzo* and *mayo*.
- **Words (14):** Januar, Februar, März, April, Mai, Juni, Juli, August, September, Oktober, November, Dezember, der Monat, der Geburtstag.
- **Warm-up (lessons 10, 21):** das Jahr, die Woche, zwölf, das Wochenende.
- **Scene:** Thursday, 9:00, room 2.04, back at the old time. Weber draws twelve columns on the board and asks everyone to write their name under their birthday month, in turn: *Wann haben Sie Geburtstag?* The learner answers in a month chosen from the learner's own profile (default: *Mai*). Ana and Lena are in *Juli* and *Oktober*. **Yuki** gives her name for the first time (Japan; she sits in the back row, takes notes in a small notebook; she writes *März* very neatly and is the first student the class hears say a whole sentence without a mistake: *Ich habe im März Geburtstag.*).
- **Who:** Weber, Ana, Lena, Yuki (named), Tomasz (late), the learner.
- **Twist:** twelve columns, six names. Yuki asks Weber *Und Sie, Herr Weber?* Weber: *Das sage ich nicht.* The class tries month by month (*Januar?* *Nein.* *Februar?* *Nein.*) until Weber says *Ach, schon gut* and writes nothing. **New fact:** Weber's birthday is a secret month; the board never shows it (payoff: lesson 54, birthdays). **Yuki** is named and from this lesson on is in the room.
- **Change from the outline:** the outline says "Weber's is in the winter" but *Winter* is lesson 42; the game is month-by-month guessing instead, which drills the twelve words. *Geburtstag* is taught here, so lesson 54 recalls it instead of introducing it; content frees one slot there.

## Lesson 23: Close family

- **Station:** Senefelderplatz. **Grammar:** *mein / dein* + a noun: *mein Bruder, meine Schwester, meine Eltern*; the ending *-e* for feminine and plural (explained as "same ending as *eine*"). *Ich habe einen Bruder.* (accusative, as in lessons 14 and 18). **ES contrast:** *mi hermano / mis hermanos*: Spanish marks only the plural, German marks the gender too (*mein Bruder / meine Schwester*); *padres* is *Eltern* only for "parents", never *Väter*; *Kind* is "child", not a kid goat.
- **Words (13):** die Familie, die Mutter, der Vater, die Eltern, der Bruder, die Schwester, die Tochter, der Sohn, das Kind, mein, dein, das Foto, zeigen.
- **Warm-up (lessons 3, 10, 22):** die Frau, der Mann, der Freund, der Geburtstag.
- **Scene:** Tuesday, 9:00, room 2.04. Weber asks everyone to bring a photo of their family and say who is who: *Das ist meine Schwester.* Ana shows her brother and her mother in Mexico City; she touches her mother's face on the phone for a second. Lena has a sister, **Giulia**, seventeen (lesson 10): *Meine Schwester ist siebzehn.* The learner picks a photo and chooses the right sentence for each person.
- **Who:** Weber, Ana, Lena, Yuki, the learner (Tomasz is late).
- **Twist:** 9:00. Weber's phone rings. This time he does not step out: he looks at the screen and shows it to the class: *Das ist meine Tochter. Clara.* The call is on the lock screen with her photo. *Sie kann warten.* (it can wait; one glossed partner phrase). He answers after the class, and the class is quiet. **New fact:** Clara is Weber's daughter, a student in Lisbon; the Tuesday call is hers (thread 1 revealed). The learner and the class know now why he moved the course to ten in lesson 20: she had an exam that week and a long call.
- **Change from the outline:** the outline had the phone ring and a reveal; here he shows the screen on purpose, so the reveal is a choice and not an accident, and *zeigen* is the first verb he gives the class.

## Lesson 24: My family (review stop)

- **Station:** Senefelderplatz (second lesson of the topic). **Grammar:** *sein / ihr / unser* (his, her, our): *sein Hund, ihre Katze, unser Kurs*; the ending follows the noun (lesson 23). *verheiratet* with *sein*: *Er ist verheiratet.* **ES contrast:** *su* covers "his, her, your (formal), their" in Spanish; German splits it into *sein* (his) and *ihr* (her), so the learner has to say whose it is; **do not** confuse *ihr* (her) with *ihr* (you plural, lesson 3).
- **Words (13):** die Oma, der Opa, der Onkel, die Tante, der Cousin, die Cousine, die Geschwister, verheiratet, der Hund, die Katze, sein, ihr, unser.
- **Warm-up (lessons 21–23):** der Montag, der Monat, die Mutter, das Foto.
- **Scene:** Thursday, 9:00. Room 2.04 is closed (new floor), so Weber says *Heute sind wir bei mir*: the whole course walks to his flat in Wedding, two rooms with photos on every wall, a cat asleep on the books, a new shelf not yet screwed to the wall. The class helps hang the photos, and everyone is asked to say whose photo it is: *Das ist ihre Katze. Das ist sein Onkel.* Ana: *Und das ist Ihre Frau?* Weber: *Nein. Meine Schwester.* (He is not married; the learner can practise *verheiratet* with *nein* and the answer is exact.)
- **Who:** the whole course (Weber, Ana, Lena, Yuki, Tomasz, the learner, plus Amira and Pedro, who stay in the background until they are named), and the cat.
- **Twist (chapter 2 ends):** Yuki sets her phone on the shelf with the timer on: one photo of the whole course in Weber's living room. Tomasz runs in at the last second, blurred; Lena is looking at her phone (*Noch nicht*, lesson 12, to herself); Weber is not looking at the camera. Weber hangs it beside Clara's photo, says nothing, then: *So.* **New fact:** the course photo hangs on Weber's wall for good; the cat is called **Kreide** (chalk).
- **Review stop:** this is the sixth review stop (after 4, 8, 12, 16, 20, 24). The scene's questions sample words from lessons 21–24, and the chapter banner for "Die Stadt" follows.
- **Change from the outline:** the outline said "the class helps Weber move the photographs"; the reason is now a closed room and a new shelf, so a teacher bringing the class home needs no explanation beyond *Der Raum ist zu*.
